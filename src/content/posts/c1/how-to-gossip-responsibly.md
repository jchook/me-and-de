title: How to Gossip Responsibly
date: 2026-08-12
level: C1
description: Der Konjunktiv I — German builds plausible deniability directly into the verb.
---

German has a verb form whose entire job is plausible deniability. It is called *der Konjunktiv I*, it is used for reported speech, and it means every sentence of German gossip ships with a built-in legal disclaimer.

*Er sagte, er sei unschuldig.* He said he was innocent — where *sei* is doing silent, load-bearing work: *these are his claims, not mine, and I am merely the courier.* English bolts this on with adverbial duct tape — "allegedly," "reportedly," "according to sources." German conjugates it. The deniability is *morphological*. German newspapers run on this tense the way engines run on fuel; libel protection is built into the grammar, which may be the most German sentence I have ever written.

Meine Nachbarin beherrscht ihn fließend:

*Der Mann vom dritten Stock habe schon wieder Besuch. Er sei angeblich Musiker. Die Geige habe allerdings noch niemand im Haus je gehört. Man wolle ja nichts sagen.*

Every verb in the subjunctive. She has asserted nothing. She has merely relayed a series of claims — visitor, alleged musician, conspicuously unheard violin — while remaining, grammatically speaking, an innocent bystander at her own gossip. *Man wolle ja nichts sagen*: one wouldn't want to say anything. In the subjunctive, one just did.

Six months ago I could not order bread.
