---
title: How to Gossip Responsibly
date: 2026-08-12
level: C1
description: Der Konjunktiv I — German builds plausible deniability directly into the verb.
---

German has created a verb form for repeating a claim while keeping one’s own hands clean. This is *der Konjunktiv I*, the form for reported speech. It marks a statement as somebody else’s: I am telling you what was said, German insists, and I decline all responsibility for its truth.

*Er sagte, er sei unschuldig.* He said he was innocent.

The little word *sei* does considerable legal and social work. It tells us that the innocence belongs to him as a claim. The speaker has merely delivered it, with the care of a person carrying a suspicious package.

English has to bolt this distance onto the sentence with words such as “allegedly” and “reportedly.” German puts it into the verb itself. This is why German press reporting relies so heavily on Konjunktiv I: a report can attribute a claim without adopting it. The language has arranged for plausible deniability to be conjugated.

My study material also supplied the possibilities for gossip:

*Der Mann vom dritten Stock habe schon wieder Besuch. Er sei angeblich Musiker. Die Geige habe allerdings noch niemand im Haus je gehört. Man wolle ja nichts sagen.*

The passage asserts nothing directly. The man on the third floor is said to have visitors. He is supposedly a musician. Nobody in the building has ever heard the violin. Each claim is held at a grammatical distance, safely marked as somebody else’s business.

Then comes the masterpiece: *Man wolle ja nichts sagen.* One would not want to say anything.

The speaker says quite a lot, but German has provided a verb form that permits her to look innocent while doing it.

I have been learning German for about six months, starting from nothing, and plan to go to Germany later this year.
