title: How to Gossip Responsibly
date: 2026-08-12
level: C1
description: Der Konjunktiv I — German builds plausible deniability directly into the verb.
---

*Der Konjunktiv I* is German’s form for reported speech. It marks a claim as somebody else’s: I am telling you what was said, not presenting it as fact myself.

*Er sagte, er sei unschuldig.* He said he was innocent. The important word is *sei*. It signals that the innocence is his claim. The speaker is only reporting it.

English usually adds that distance with words such as “allegedly” and “reportedly.” German puts it in the verb. This is why German press reporting relies so heavily on Konjunktiv I: it lets a report attribute claims without adopting them.

Today’s study notes included the possibilities for gossip:

*Der Mann vom dritten Stock habe schon wieder Besuch. Er sei angeblich Musiker. Die Geige habe allerdings noch niemand im Haus je gehört. Man wolle ja nichts sagen.*

The speaker asserts nothing herself. She relays that the man on the third floor has visitors, that he is supposedly a musician, and that nobody has heard his violin. Every claim remains grammatically at a distance.

*Man wolle ja nichts sagen.* One would not want to say anything. The Konjunktiv I makes that position available even while saying quite a lot.
