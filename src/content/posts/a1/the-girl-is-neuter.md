---
title: The Girl Is Neuter
date: 2026-07-05
level: A1
description: On German grammatical gender, and the specific tragedy of das Mädchen.
---

German nouns have three grammatical genders: masculine, feminine, and neuter. They do not reliably describe what the noun is.

*Der Löffel. Die Gabel. Das Messer.* The spoon is masculine, the fork is feminine, and the knife is neuter. The sun is feminine and the moon is masculine, the opposite of French.

Then there is *das Mädchen*: the girl. Neuter.

This follows a rule. Anything ending in *-chen* is a diminutive, and German diminutives are neuter. That rule takes priority over the natural gender of the person being described.

It also affects the pronoun. *Das Mädchen liest. Es liest.* The girl reads. It reads.

Mark Twain filed the definitive complaint in 1880:

> In German, a young lady has no sex, while a turnip has. Think what overwrought reverence that shows for the turnip, and what callous disrespect for the girl.

My textbook's advice is to memorize the gender with every noun. That means learning the article along with the word: *der Tisch*, *die Tür*, *das Mädchen*.

*Der Tisch ist ein Mann. Die Tür ist eine Frau. Ich bin müde.*
