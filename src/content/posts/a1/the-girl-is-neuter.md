---
title: Why German Calls a Girl “It”
date: 2026-07-05
level: A1
description: On German grammatical gender, and the specific tragedy of das Mädchen.
---

German nouns have three grammatical genders: masculine, feminine, and neuter. The system does not reliably describe what the noun is, which is an arrangement that gives the learner plenty to memorize and very little comfort.

*Der Löffel. Die Gabel. Das Messer.* The spoon is masculine, the fork is feminine, and the knife is neuter. The sun is feminine and the moon is masculine, the opposite of French. German has assigned these offices and does not appear interested in explaining itself.

Then comes *das Mädchen*: the girl. Neuter.

There is, at least, a rule behind this particular indignity. Anything ending in *-chen* is a diminutive, and German diminutives are neuter. The ending takes priority over the natural gender of the person being described.

It takes priority over the pronoun, too. *Das Mädchen liest. Es liest.* The girl reads. It reads.

Mark Twain filed the definitive complaint in 1880:

> In German, a young lady has no sex, while a turnip has. Think what overwrought reverence that shows for the turnip, and what callous disrespect for the girl.

My textbook's advice is to memorize the gender with every noun. This is sensible advice, though it feels less like learning vocabulary than accepting German's terms. Learn the article with the word: *der Tisch*, *die Tür*, *das Mädchen*.

*Der Tisch ist ein Mann. Die Tür ist eine Frau. Ich bin müde.*
